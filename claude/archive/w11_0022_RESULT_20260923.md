# Part 1 (step 1) — Strategy-sourcing methodology: reuse in W05 / W06 / W07

2026-09-23 · Opus session on W11-0022 (Project management chat) · Judgement only. Nothing built, nothing backtested, no P&L.
Source material: `claude/yt_collation_ai_trading_bots_and_strategy_sourcing_20260923.md` (video 1, Miles Deutscher, method credited to DaviddTech).

## In plain terms

The video's four "free strategy sources" are really two kinds of thing. TradingView community scripts and the Stonehill/NNFX indicator library give you **indicators, not strategies**. Whoever converts them has to invent the stops, targets and exits, and that inventing is where a backtest gets fitted. The project already turned this route down on 2026-09-19, when it reviewed DaviddTech's own video (DaviddTech is the person this method is credited to). QuantConnect's leaderboard ranks thousands of community strategies by their **last three months' return**, so its top rows are the luckiest results of a very large search, not the best strategies. **Quantpedia is the one worth using.** It is an index of published academic strategies, which is what the literature pipeline (W08) and the swing line (W07) already draw on. Use it only to find the paper. The numbers always come from the paper itself.

## Verdict — what Ben must decide

- **No decision is needed from step 1.** Nothing is bought and no item is raised yet. Step 4 decides whether anything becomes a task.
- **Take one thing:** Quantpedia's free tier (about 70 well-known strategies) as a **lookup index** for W08 intake and for W07's two remaining hypotheses. It is a pointer to the paper and never the source of a number. Cost $0.
- **Refuse three:** TradingView community scripts, the Stonehill/NNFX indicator library, and the QuantConnect leaderboard ranking, as sources of candidates for W05, W06 or W07. The reasons are below.
- **Don't buy Quantpedia Premium/Pro now.** The pipeline hasn't yet processed its first four candidates, all on data already held (W08 spec §6). Paying for more candidates before it has processed any is backwards.
- **Proposed input to W08-0001** (Ben's pending decision on the pipeline spec): add one line to Stage 0/1 that makes the rule above explicit. Posted as an Update on W08-0001.

## 2. Why each source is taken or refused

### 2.1 TradingView community scripts: refused

1. **An indicator is not a strategy.** The video had Claude rewrite indicators into strategies "as true to the original as possible". Every stop, target and exit it added is a free choice. The pipeline refuses exactly this at Stage 1 ("construction not fully specified in public"), and Breitstein's ORB was the precedent.
2. **Editors' Picks and Trending are chosen for popularity and good charts**, not for any trading record.
3. **TradingView bars have no order inside the bar.** The DaviddTech review (§16.5) already found that this makes a TradingView backtest weaker evidence than what the project runs, and the project bought one-second bars for this reason (`orb_sip_RESOLVED`).
4. **The project's own record with this route:** TL-v0, built from Tori Trades' method, matched her one checkable trade **0 of 1** (`tl_v0_fidelity_RESULT_20260920`). TAI's SMA-Fib v2 is parked behind TSMOM (W01-0009).
5. Some community scripts **repaint**: their plotted signals use information from later bars. That would have to be checked script by script before any of them was trusted.

### 2.2 Stonehill Forex / NNFX indicator library: refused

- NNFX is a **forex, daily-chart framework with five slots**: a baseline, two confirmations, a volume indicator and an exit indicator. Each slot is filled from a library of hundreds of indicators, so the number of combinations is itself a search, and a large one. That is the opposite of the project's rule that searches are counted by family.
- Stonehill's per-indicator results are published as **screenshots**. The result pages don't state pairs, dates or costs; they point to a separate settings page and a course.
- Ben has **no forex workstream**. The confluence strategies the project has already built (MCL "momentum confluence", RSI/MFI mean reversion) are the family that failed.

### 2.3 QuantConnect leaderboard: refused as a source, with one possible use

- QuantConnect's "out-of-sample" means **performance since the author submitted the strategy**. The leaderboard ranks by **three-month return and one-year Sharpe**, with only a score penalty for less than a year of that record. Sorting many strategies by recent return puts the lucky ones on top.
- A one-year live record can't separate skill from luck. The Kevin Davey verdict (W01-0003) judged 6–12 months too short.
- The code can be cloned (Python/C# on QuantConnect's LEAN engine). Porting it to Ben's `ib_async` stack is real work.
- **The possible use:** QuantConnect and Quantpedia cooperate, and QuantConnect hosts implementations of Quantpedia strategies. For a W08 candidate, that is a **second, independent implementation** to compare against the project's own code. It could help with the pipeline's §3.3 harness check. It is a cross-check, not a result, and only worth doing if a candidate's replication check (H-R) fails.

### 2.4 Quantpedia: taken, as an index only

- Quantpedia's own pages give **~70 free strategies** ("well-known in the quant community") and a paid set they describe as both "600+" and "900+". Out-of-sample backtests are Pro tier only.
- The free tier is the well-known, most-studied anomalies. They are the ones most likely to have **replicated and then decayed**, which is the pipeline's own registered expectation for its modal outcome (W08 spec §8). That makes them a cheap, decisive test, not a likely edge.
- The paid tier is described as "lesser-known or unique". That means less replicated and a weaker prior, at a price. Not now.

## 3. Two claims in the video that don't hold up

- **"Claude picked five strategies out of sample."** It didn't. The model has read the papers and their published backtests, so its picks are chosen with the results already in view.
- **50 strategies × several assets = 325+ backtests, unregistered.** At a 5% bar, about 16 of 325 would look good by chance alone. Step 2 of W11-0022 deals with this in full. It's noted here only because it's the sourcing method's own weak point.

## 4. What carries over

- The presenter's own after-candidate workflow (backtest, then chart replay, then paper forward test, then small live, then scale) is the same shape as the pipeline's forward-test queue (W08 spec §5.1). Nothing new.
- His advice to **curate a handful rather than mass-test** agrees with the pipeline's cost-per-decision ordering.
- DaviddTech's own figure is still the best frame: **"backtested over 2,000 strategies … currently trading seven"** (0.35% survival, `source_videos_16_daviddtech_20260919` §16.6).

# Part 2 (step 2) — The 50-strategy backtest in video 1: is it sound enough to trust any headline?

2026-09-23 · Opus, same session as Part 1 · Judgement from the transcript summary. Nothing re-run.

## In plain terms

No. It is a search, not a test. Fifty strategies were picked from four sources, run more than 325 times across different coins, stocks and timeframes, and then the good-looking ones were chosen **after** the results were on screen. Nothing was fixed in advance. At a normal 5% bar, about 16 of 325 runs would look good by luck alone. So a handful of winners is exactly what an empty search produces. Most of the tests were on Bitcoin and Ethereum through a strong crypto bull market, where almost any long-biased rule looks good. The best "sustainable" picks were two Bitcoin daily strategies with profit factors of 1.78 and 1.54 and **no trade counts given**, so it is impossible to tell whether they are evidence or noise.

## Verdict

**Not usable as evidence of anything.** Nothing from it is carried forward, and nothing is re-run. It is logged here so it isn't proposed again, the same treatment the DaviddTech video got on 2026-09-19.

## Against the project's own checklist (`PROGRAM_INDEX` §4)

| Check | This backtest |
| --- | --- |
| Rules and pass bar written before running | **No.** Selection after seeing the results ("flag the most sustainable") |
| How many things were tried, counted | **No.** 50 strategies × assets × timeframes = 325+ runs; ~16 false passes expected at 5% |
| Costs at three levels | Not stated in video 1 |
| Both halves of the period positive | Not shown |
| Remove the best 1/3/5 names or periods and re-check | Not shown |
| A real held-back period | **No.** The "127% out-of-sample" figure is QuantConnect's own leaderboard number, not his run. "Claude's own 5 picks" aren't out of sample either: the model already knows the published backtests |
| Enough trades to judge | Unknown for the top picks. One candidate had **2–4 trades in 6 years** (the presenter himself said too few) |
| Comparison to just holding | Only against BTC buy-and-hold, over a period BTC rose strongly |
| Drawdown taken seriously | The headline +3,447% came with a **64% drawdown**; Claude itself flagged it as unusable |

## What it gets right

- The presenter says a backtest only proves the past and that a paper forward test is the real check. That matches the pipeline's forward-test queue (W08 spec §5.1).
- He says mass-testing burned a lot of money and advises curating a handful. That agrees with ordering by cost per decision.

# Part 3 (step 3) — Freqtrade, Vibe Trading and NoFX against Ben's setup

## In plain terms

None of the three can trade through IBKR, and none handles CME futures in the way the project uses them. **Freqtrade** is crypto-only. **NoFX** is crypto exchanges only, and in NoFX the language model *is* the strategy, so there is nothing fixed to register or backtest. **Vibe Trading** is the only one that connects to IBKR at all, and that link is **read-only**. It can place paper and live orders through Alpaca and others, but its data comes from free sources such as Yahoo, and its "alpha library" is mostly large formula collections, which are a search in disguise. Ben's own trader already has the plumbing these projects advertise: order lifecycle, risk limits, pause and stop from the portal. It also carries fixes for IBKR-specific failures (the MEDS ghost, the CRML double sell) that none of these would have.

## Verdict

**Adopt none. Adapt nothing.** No installs.

## Framework by framework

- **Freqtrade: discard.** Crypto-only. Its dry-run mode and Telegram control are things Ben already has: IBKR paper trading, the cloud portal and `notify.py`. Moving the trader onto it would mean leaving IBKR.
- **Vibe Trading: discard, with one note.** Its 456-alpha library is 154 Qlib + 101 "Formulaic Alphas" + 191 Guotai Junan formulas + 10 academic ones. The first three are **446 formulas to search through**: the multiple-comparisons problem again. The 10 academic ones include Jegadeesh reversal (what SWING-v0 tests) and George–Hwang 52-week high (W08 queue #8). They are simple formulas the project writes itself, and on free data they run without a point-in-time universe, which is what swing's G8 gate was built to fix. Not worth the install for a second opinion on a one-line formula.
- **NoFX: discard.** A strategy that is a language model deciding live can't be registered, backtested or reproduced. That is incompatible with every standard in `PROGRAM_INDEX` §4. It also only reaches crypto venues.

## The one thing checked on Ben's side

Vibe Trading advertises a "mandate" (symbol list, order size, exposure, daily cap), a kill switch, a fail-closed pre-trade check and an audit ledger. Ben's `brokers/ibkr/trader.py` already has, from the portal, a pause that stops new entries while still managing open positions, a stop, per-position stops, the 6% drift guard, the $2–20 price band, the position cap and the fill log. Exposure is bounded by construction: flat 100 shares × at most $20 × 3 positions ≈ $6,000. There is **no account-level daily-loss stop in the live path**. That is deliberate: the project tested it as a rule (H-S4 / H-S6) and did not bolt it on. **Nothing to borrow.**
