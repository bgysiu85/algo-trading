### 0.3 The leak was two leaks, and the one we audited was the wrong one

in two on the same tape, same slices, same dates. At $4.26:

| | MCL | MC5 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Stage-2 | −5.86/trade (2,656) | −6.37/trade (5,854) |
| Point-in-time, **no floor** | −3.65/trade (4,378) | −2.25/trade (9,137) |
| Point-in-time, **floored** | **−10.49/trade** (2,682) | **−14.25/trade** (5,451) |
| **Universe leak** | **−2.20** | **−4.11** |
| **Intraday leak** | **+6.84** | **+11.99** |

**The universe leak is NEGATIVE for both strategies.** Choosing names with the session's own
daily bar in hand made results *worse*. Stage 2's RVOL and day-range filter — the entire target
of the audit — selected slightly **unfavourable** names relative to what the live pre-market
screen would have surfaced.

**The whole leak, and more, is the intraday one: buying a name before the screen would have shown
it.** The published runs turned the engine loose over 04:00–09:30 on names chosen for the day, so
it could enter at 04:05 on a name that would not appear on the watchlist until 07:20. Footprint:
**27.0% of MCL symbol-days, 68.8% of MC5's**. The floor removes **39% of MCL's trades and 40% of
MC5's**.

> **The live trader has never had this defect.** The feed surfaces a name when it surfaces it.
> So this is a concrete, quantified piece of the execution gap: the backtests were flattered by
> **$6.84 (MCL) to $11.99 (MC5) per trade of timing live trading cannot reproduce.**
> `execution_gap_20260910.md` documented that gap as a mystery. Part of it now has a number.

**This supersedes `pit_h0` §2's conclusion that "the universe — not the rule — was the edge."**
For the strategies that is now wrong. The correct statement is **"the timing, not the universe,
was the edge — and it was an edge only in the backtest."** The H0 case is **untested rather than
confirmed**: `pit_h0` has no unfloored arm and never split its own leak.

## 3. The structural questions, if the loss does not close

Not parameter changes. Both are currently closed off, and both are what the external evidence
points at.

**Posted limit entries instead of marketable.** Four independent literatures converge — Taiwan's
entire day-trader population, Garvey & Murphy's profitable professionals (64% of volume passive),
FINRA's 27-million-order dataset (limit beats marketable by **~20 bps in small caps**), and the
reversal literature. Needs a fill model (~60% fill rate, adverse selection on the fills you get).
`stop_fill` now supplies the first per-leg inputs. **Build the fill model once and it serves the
stop-fill question too.**

**The short side.** `short_selling_feasibility.md` answered no at IBKR. Barber et al. identify
willingness to short as one of two distinguishing features of the profitable minority, and this
universe is the most short-constrained corner of the market. **Long-only is a cost being paid,
not a design that was validated.**

## 4. Still unmodelled

1. **Halts.** Nothing models LULD. Neither `screen_sim` nor `pit_h0` handles a halted symbol-day.
   36.4% of Tier 2 pauses fall 09:45–09:50 ET; Tier 2 pauses occur on 100% of trading days.
2. **Survivorship in the narrow sense.** The universe-selection leak is measured; nothing checks
   whether halted, reverse-split or delisted names are present. Daily bars are not split-adjusted
   — a 1-for-10 prints as a ~900% gain.
3. **Posted-limit fill rate and adverse selection** on this universe. The ~60% / ~20bps are
   external figures.
4. **Market impact at retail size in illiquid names.** Unstudied anywhere. ~1,450 shares average
   against a book with <$5,000 displayed within a cent of the mid.
5. **`window_close` as a priced leg.** Worst of the three, lands on the longest-held trades.

## 7. The decision point

MCL beating its control on both denominators is the first structurally positive finding in this
project, and it should be given its due. Selection is doing something real.

It is also **−$10.49/trade and −$6.23 gross**, every arm of both strategies is negative at every
friction level, and "never trade" beats all of it.

Against that: break-even friction is negative on the screened universe; five `bar_cache`-class
findings have inverted; and the external literature finds the adjacent effects die on costs in
stocks with spreads **10–100× tighter** than these.

The honest read is that the project now knows, with unusual precision, **how far short it is and
why** — and that the remaining levers that have not been tested are structural (§3), not
parametric. That is a better position than it was in this morning, and it is still a losing one.
