# Stop-fill slippage, re-measured from 5 live sessions

**2026-09-11.** `friction_reconciliation_20260911.md` §4 names the stop-fill cost
as *"the largest un-nailed number in the project"* — n=2, measured on a session
that was 81% apex exits, applied to a configuration that now produces ~95%
trailing exits. §5 item 4 asks for a re-measurement once live sessions
accumulate. There are now five.

Source: `var/fills/mcl_fills_2026090{2,3,8,9}.csv` and `..._20260910.csv`,
151 rows, 102 filled. `slippage_vs_ref` is **per share** (`fill − ref`;
verified: 12.17 − 12.83 = −0.66).

## The measurement

| leg | n | median | mean | p10 | worst |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BUY entry | 51 | +0.0007 | +0.0028 | −0.0800 | −0.3300 |
| **SELL trailing_stop** | **21** | **−0.0225** | **−0.0846** | −0.2200 | −0.6600 |
| SELL apex_reversal | 23 | −0.0095 | −0.0182 | −0.0600 | −0.1300 |
| SELL window_close | 7 | −0.0700 | −0.0946 | −0.5000 | −0.5000 |

**20 of 21 trailing stops filled adverse.** This is not a two-sided
distribution with a bit of noise; it is one-sided with a long left tail.

Per 100 shares, round trip (buy + trailing-stop sell):

```
median   -$2.18        shipped MEASURED_FRICTION   -$4.26
mean     -$8.18        live end-to-end, n=6        -$8.92
```

## Why the mean is the number that matters

Median −$2.25 and mean −$8.46 on the sell leg is a 3.8× gap — the cost is
concentrated in a few bad fills (−$0.66, −$0.39, −$0.22 per share). **You pay
the mean, not the median.** A backtest charging a flat per-trade number is
charging an average, so the average is what it should be charged.

**The mean converges with the one independent check.** `mcl_session_20260909_review.md`
measured **−$8.92/RT end-to-end** on 6 round trips by a completely different
route — comparing actual session P/L against the backtest. This measurement,
from order-level quote logging across 21 trailing stops, lands at −$8.18 to
−$8.46. Two methods, two samples, the same answer.

That is real corroboration, and it is worth contrasting with a false one: the
EQUS.MINI and XNAS.BASIC per-trade figures agree within $0.75 while **sharing
only 16.4% of their trades** (`tape_compare`, 2026-09-11). Agreement between
measurements of the same quantity by different routes is evidence; agreement
between two different trade sets is arithmetic.

> **The shipped $4.26 understates stop-fill cost by roughly half.** It sits
> between this median and this mean, and the reason is that it was measured on
> apex exits, which are genuinely cheaper (−$0.0095/share median against the
> trail's −$0.0225).

## What this changes, and what it does not

**Does not change any verdict.** `friction_reconciliation_20260911.md` §3
already showed break-even friction is **negative** on the screened universe for
all three strategies — they lose *gross*, before any cost assumption. Repricing
MCL at $8.92 takes it from −$23,394 to −$44,275; it was never going to cross
zero.

**Does change the honest error bar.** Every `bar_cache` figure quoted at $4.26
should now be read with the $8.92 column of that doc's sensitivity table as the
central case, not the pessimistic one.

## Caveats

- **21 trailing stops across 5 sessions.** Better than n=2 on the wrong
  mechanic; not a large sample.
- **These are paper fills.** IBKR simulates them. The dispersion is real
  (+$0.03 to −$0.66 per share), so the simulator is not pinning fills to the
  limit — but whether it is optimistic or pessimistic against real capital is
  unmeasured, and cannot be measured without real capital.
- **`window_close` is the worst leg of all** (−$0.0700/share median, −$0.0946
  mean, n=7). The 09:30 forced exit pays more than the trail does. Nothing has
  ever priced that separately, and `hold_cap_decision.md`'s finding that the
  best bucket is 60+ bars means the window-close exits are on the trades that
  ran — the ones worth most.
