      favourable fill. The execution-quality metric scored the worst trade in
      the book as one of the best fills of the day. That is the project's
      recurring shape again -- a control whose output is indistinguishable
      from the failure it is meant to detect.

  (b) THE TRAILING STOP CANNOT CHASE A COLLAPSE.
      The stop is a LIMIT order priced just under the current bid. In a fast
      fall the bid moves faster than the loop, so the first attempt was stale
      (9.06 against a bid that had gone) and the next three were priced BELOW
      what IBKR would accept. Five attempts, 24 seconds, during a 42% drop.
      Every other exit on both days filled on the first attempt.

      "We cannot accept an order at a limit price at or more aggressive than

3. IS "THE REFERENCE HAS ALREADY MOVED" A USABLE FILTER? NO.
-------------------------------------------------------------

The obvious response to 2(a) is to refuse entries whose quote has drifted from
the signal close. That was tested across the whole live book before writing it
down: 138 paired round trips, drift = (mid at order / signal close - 1).

    Q1  drift [ (8.59%) .. (1.91%)]  n=34   net (414.47)   per (12.19)  win 12%
    Q2  drift [ (1.63%) .. (0.24%)]  n=35   net    23.40   per    0.67  win 26%
    Q3  drift [ (0.24%) ..   0.44% ]  n=34   net (321.94)   per  (9.47)  win 15%
    Q4  drift [   0.46% ..  10.54% ]  n=35   net (490.12)   per (14.00)  win 14%

  NOT MONOTONE, and the only positive bucket is an interior one -- which is
  what noise looks like. Removing the single CRBP trade takes Q1 from (12.19)
  to (5.54), so nearly the whole Q1 effect IS that one trade.

  VERDICT: drift does not separate outcomes in this book. It belongs as a RISK
  CONTROL on order placement -- do not send a buy into a quote 6%+ below the
  reference -- not as an entry filter. Those are different claims and only the
  first is supported.

  Median drift is (0.24%); p10 (5.39%); p90 2.13%.

7. WHAT TO DO
--------------

Ranked by what the evidence here actually supports.

  1. READ THE BROKER'S PRICE BAND OUT OF THE REJECTION AND USE IT. IBKR states
     the acceptable limit in the text of the rejection. Three of 09-14's eight
     failed orders were re-sends that ignored a bound the broker had already
     given. Cheap, mechanical, no strategy question.

  2. A STOP THAT CANNOT FILL IS NOT A STOP. The 5% trail behaved correctly on
     50 of 51 exits across these two days and failed on the one occasion it
     mattered. Either the stop leg becomes marketable once price is through
     the level by some margin, or the trail's measured cost has to carry the
     tail it does not currently show. Do not change TRAIL_PCT -- that lever is
     closed; this is about the ORDER TYPE, which is section 2.5 of
     intraday_candidates_20260915.md and was already ranked.

  3. STOP SCORING FILLS BY slippage_vs_ref ALONE. It called (241.37) a
     59-cent favourable fill. A buy filling far BELOW its reference is a
     warning that the reference is stale, not a bargain. The metric needs a
     sign-aware companion -- drift at order time -- reported beside it.

  4. RANK, DO NOT LET ARRIVAL ORDER DECIDE. 58 cap rejections, and on 09-14
     the cap's accidental choices were better than the strategy's deliberate
     ones. This is item 3 in the proposed PROGRAM_INDEX §7 insert and the
     live record keeps arguing for it.

  5. Run the placement instrument. Section 5 establishes the labelled set is
     about the same decisions the live system is making.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SUPPORT

  - It does not support a regime-change reading. Ex-CRBP the two days are
    (7.42)/trade against a (6.87) baseline.
  - It does not support a drift entry filter. Section 3, explicitly tested.
  - n=39 over two sessions, and 16.7% win rate over 138 trades total. The
    sample is SESSIONS, and there are eight of them.
