## 14.2 The grading system — and this project has already run his own validation step

`ubofAZwgd4w`. He grades every trade **A / B / C / D** before entry and sizes on
the grade. D = no trade (*"literally 99.9% of charts are D grade or worse"*),
C = feeler size, B = standard (*"boring pays the bills"*), A = size up.

**He is explicit that the rubric is not portable:** *"These grades… it's all
subjective, completely subjective. My A is not your A trade… The point is not to
copy my rubric. The point is to have a rubric."* So there is nothing to lift.

**What is portable is his step three, and it is this project's method:**

> *"After a few months, go back and compare your grades to your results. **Are
> your A trades actually outperforming your B trades?** If yes, your rubric is
> real and you should be sizing more aggressively on your A trades. **If no, your
> criteria are wrong.**"*

**This project has already run that test, at a scale no discretionary journal
reaches, and the answer was no.**

| study | scope | result |
|---|---|---|
| `entry_features_result_20260912` | 22 features, 11 families, 88 buckets | **0 clear** |
| `entry_margins_result_20260914` | 112 buckets | **zero positive** |
| whole-tape census | best volatility-independent feature | **1.94×** against a **0.108%** base rate |
| break-even requirement | — | **1.86× lift; best measured 1.56×** |

**That closes the question flagged on 2026-09-09.** When his exponential sizing
was first recorded, the note read: *"this only works if you can differentiate
trade quality ex ante… That precondition is a testable question, not a matter of
opinion. Does our signal carry information about magnitude, or only about
direction? **Check before adopting.**"*

**It was checked. On this universe, on 3,955 trades, the rubric is not real.**
Variable sizing is therefore not justified here, and MCL's flat 100 shares is
correct — not by default, but by measurement. **Record this as answered, and do
not reopen exponential sizing for this universe without new features.**

**Two secondary points worth keeping.**

*A separate rubric per setup.* *"You cannot grade a capitulation trade with a
breakout rubric… The variables that make a panic sell-off an A+ are almost the
opposite of the variables that make a breakout an A+."* Every feature study in
this project tests one feature set across a whole book. That is a real structural
criticism, and it is a reason the 88 buckets may have been asking one question of
two populations — though `entry_excursion`'s "drawdown first / move first" split
already does some of this work.

*His capitulation rubric inverts our universe.* He wants **a boring, low-beta
name making a violent move** — *"boring stock, violent move, that's the combo"* —
because the move is then more likely an overreaction. **Our screen selects names
that move violently every day**, which by his own logic is the population where
the signal means least. Logged alongside §9.7's structural mismatch, which made
the same kind of point about Nill's range premise.

## 14.3 The ORB video confirms that V3's blanks are real, not an artefact

`QmPUp9ISuDw`, a dedicated ten-minute ORB video from the source recorded in
`orb_strategy_spec.md` as *stop: not stated, target: not stated*.

**He still states neither.** Range definition is confirmed — first 30 minutes,
**wick of the high to wick of the low** — and nothing else is specified. Nobody
should go looking for his stop rule; it is not in public.

**Three use cases, which are context classifications rather than rules:**

1. **Exhaustion gap** — a huge gap up after an already-vertical run; the late
   buyers are trapped, and the break *down* out of the opening range is the
   trade. His examples: SMCI, MSTR.
2. **Continuation** — day one after a material catalyst (earnings, FDA, guidance).
   The range is a launchpad because *"institutional money often cannot fully
   position immediately"*.
3. **Macro panic or euphoria on index instruments** — 2025 tariff panic, 2020
   COVID. *"Everyone is watching the same levels."*

**Four stated nuances**, in his order: consolidation quality inside the range;
**volume confirmation on the break** (*"weak volume breakouts are far more likely
to fail"*); **market context**; and risk management over setup.

**The one testable item, and it is nearly free.** Market context appears in
*both* this video and the breakout rubric of §14.2, and there he weights it as
decisive: *"This one variable can single-handedly downgrade an A setup to a C or
worse."* An ORB long in a downtrending market against one in risk-on.

`orb_sip_RESOLVED_20260918` leaves a **resolved ledger of 7,239 top-20 trades**
(917,628 across both range lengths) on disk. Adding SPY's state as a column and
re-splitting costs **no new data and no re-run of the strategy**.

**But read the prior first.** ORB SIP closed at **1 of 7**, and the binding
failure is criterion 1: the resolved book is **+4.8R total**, and dropping the
single best symbol takes it to **(52.1)R**. **A context gate does not fix
concentration**, and the honest expectation is that splitting by market state
produces two concentrated halves rather than one clean one.

**One observation that outranks the video.** ORB SIP is the **only line in this
project with a positive gross** — **+0.351R against friction of 0.350R**.
Everything else (MCL (6.26)/trade, MC5, VW9, SPY intraday (0.308) bps) is
negative *before any cost*. So the argument in
`spy_intraday_AMENDMENT_A_20260917` §A.5 — that venue and friction cannot rescue
a negative gross — **does not apply to ORB SIP.** It is the one place where a
cost reduction or a conditioning rule could in principle matter. That should be
recorded before the line is written off.

## 14.4 The small-account challenge — a direct counterpoint to §13, from an audited source

`LbSn-awZmm4`, published a month before Cameron's 46-day video. It answers Ben's
2026-09-17 question independently of anything said in this project.

> *"There has never been a successful trader at a legitimate proprietary trading
> firm who voluntarily decided to slash their buying power by 99% just to see if
> they could run up a tiny account. It did not happen at Trillium. It does not
> happen at SMB Capital. And it doesn't happen at Jane Street."*

**His three arguments, and each is checkable rather than rhetorical:**

**(a) The incentive.** *"The challenge isn't designed because it's the best way to
trade. It's designed because it's the best way to capture attention… a
responsible 20% return on a $2,000 account does not go viral. The creator is
forced to take oversized risk, concentrate positions, use excessive leverage, or
swing for home runs."* This is §13.4's arithmetic from the other side — where that
section showed Cameron's cushion was 20 points of accuracy at zero commission,
this explains why the format demands the risk profile it does.

**(b) Scalability, which is the substantive one.** *"Turning 2K into 50K tells me
almost nothing about whether they can manage serious capital… these accounts are
compounded using leverage, huge portfolio exposure, and styles that don't have
the liquidity to scale."* **This project has measured exactly that**:
`sizing_and_capacity.md` found capital-based sizing flips MCL's sign
(+$0.33/trade flat-100 → −$6,158 at 60% of $5,000 buying power). A strategy that
works at 100 shares and dies at size is the failure he is describing, and we have
it in our own data.

**(c) The revealed preference.** *"Why would I spend months trying to make 40K
when I could spend that same time making 400K or 4 million? The only scenario
where that makes financial sense is if being an influencer pays more than being a
trader."*

**It corroborates the reframe in `research_session_close_20260918` §5** — *prove
the process compounds, don't chase the income* — from a named practitioner with
an audited record rather than from this chat's arithmetic. **Two independent
routes to the same conclusion is why it is recorded here.**

## 14.5 Reading the tape — the one video with computable content

`RKV1rncXSkg`. This is the most directly useful of the four, because the build
chat has **order-book imbalance queued as item 3** with **IBKR TotalView
purchased** and **Databento MBP-1 for twelve months**, and this names what the
footprints actually look like.

**Three order types and their detectable signatures:**

| type | what it does | footprint, and whether MBP-1 can see it |
|---|---|---|
| **displayed** | full size shown | offer disappears *and* a matching print lands — directly observable |
| **refreshing / iceberg** | shows a slice, reloads at the same price | **repeated executions at one price on one venue that far exceed the displayed quantity** — computable as cumulative executed volume at a level against displayed size |
| **hidden** | no display at all | **executions at a level with little or no visible size** — same computation, different sign |

**The aggression ladder**, which is the thing he says tape reading actually
measures: passive limit → improving the bid → **taking the offer** → market
order. *"Reading the tape is largely about judging the relative aggressiveness of
buyers and sellers. Are buyers repeatedly lifting offers? Are sellers
aggressively hitting bids?"* **That is trade-sign classification, and MBP-1
carries the aggressor side directly** — no Lee-Ready inference needed.

**Two data traps worth carrying into the registration:**

- **Size is displayed in lots, not shares.** *"One lot represents 100 shares. If
  you see the number five next to the bid, it represents 500 shares, not just
  five."* A feed read as shares is wrong by 100×, and the error would look
  plausible.
- **Level 2 shows visible liquidity only**, and dark-pool prints appear in time
  and sales but never on the book. This corroborates the build chat's own
  registration note that *displayed size is not committed size* — from the
  practitioner side.

**And he states the scope limit himself, which the registration should adopt:**

> *"Reading the tape is useless in most situations, except for very specific
> moments in in-play tickers… peak capitulations, big breakout moments, and
> instances of big breaking news headlines."*

**So the claim is conditional, and testing it across a whole book would test
something he is not claiming.** That is a materially better registration than
"does order-book imbalance predict outcome", and it is his own framing rather
than a concession extracted from him.

## 14.6 Standing on this source

**A method and calibration source, not a strategy source** — the same category as
IRONCLAD (§8) and Graystone (§11).

**Take:**

- §14.2's step three, **as an answered question**: the rubric was tested on this
  universe and is not real. Exponential sizing stays closed for this universe.
- §14.5's three footprints, the aggression ladder, the lots trap and the
  conditional scope, **into the order-book registration** when it is written.
- §14.4 as independent corroboration of the capital reframe.
- §14.3's market-context split **only** with its prior stated — it does not fix
  concentration.

**Do not take:** his A–D thresholds (he says himself they are subjective and
non-portable), his ORB stop or target (he states neither, in a dedicated ORB
video), or the $100m credential as verified until the Market Wizards profile
exists and can be read.

**And note what §14.2 did to this file.** Of the ideas catalogued in §3 as "new
to this project", exponential sizing was one of the most corroborated — four
independent arrivals at risk-normalised sizing, plus his own prop-desk argument.
**It has now been closed by measurement rather than by argument**, and the thing
that closed it was his own stated validation step. That is the best outcome a
source in this collection can produce.
